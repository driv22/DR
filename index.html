Model. Coach. Care.

Microsoft defines the manager role this way. It was already how I worked before the title caught up, which is the only reason I trust it. Underneath it: clarity, ownership, trust.

Model

Show what good looks like

  • Stay close to the work: discovery, strategy, customer conversations. Not the sidelines.
  • Out-prepare the room, and find the real question behind the ask.
  • Connect the technology to a business outcome. Be the trusted advisor, not the feature.
  • Stay a learn-it-all. Hands on with agents, Copilot Studio, and whatever is next.

Coach

Make great work repeatable

  • Coach the real gap in the moment, to help win. Not to critique after.
  • Build demo-review rhythms and discovery playbooks so a win repeats instead of resetting.
  • Debrief the losses as carefully as the wins, so the lesson spreads past the person who learned it.
  • Raise the bar on real working examples. No Mickey Mouse demos.

Care

High bar, people first

  • Air cover with a high bar. Wins loud, feedback private.
  • Build a connected team with no silos, partnering early across account executives, specialists, and engineers.
  • Develop engineers the way I already do it: alongside them, on live work.
  • Keep quick wins on the board, so the long transformation stays survivable.

Where the twenty-six years actually start. Predates this system, and is not filed here.

I founded the Financial Services Copilot Council. Two hundred and eighty-one executives later, I co-chair it.

Senior AI leaders and C-suite executives from across the industry, on a regular cadence. The point was never a vendor briefing. Microsoft can already book those.

The point is that the people carrying this problem inside their own institution get to compare notes with the people carrying it inside a competing one: what actually shipped, what quietly failed, what the regulator asked on the second call. Adoption moves faster when the peer group is real. That has been true every time.

Systems in scope.

What the team is accountable for inside a regulated institution. Four modes, usually in this order: set the technical strategy, build the demo and the proof of concept, drive adoption from pilot to production, then stay the advisor they call after the paperwork is signed.

SystemWhat the work isDepth
Microsoft 365 CopilotReadiness · governance · scale Getting from a license count to a habit. In a bank the demo is not the hard part. The compliance review, the data boundary, and the rollout sequence are. Lead
Copilot ChatWorkforce entry point Where most of an organization actually meets AI. Measured on usage depth and monthly active use, not on seats invoiced. Lead
Copilot Studio & agentsAgent365 · governance Custom agents against real financial workflows, built where the data and the controls already live, then secured, measured, and placed against the rest of the platform. Lead
Microsoft TeamsPhone · Premium · Rooms Regulated-grade collaboration: recording, retention, and eDiscovery that survive an audit, and rooms that work on a trading floor as well as in a boardroom. Deep
Employee Experience & VivaFrontline included Reaching the people who never sit at a desk, and the ones who sit at one but have never opened the intranet. Deep
CompetitiveOpenAI · Anthropic · Google · Zoom · Cisco · Slack I would rather win on the integration and the compliance story than on a feature grid. Feature grids change monthly; a data boundary does not. Active

Where I think this goes

  1. From licenses to consumptionThe scoreboard moves to monthly active use, usage depth, business impact, and FinOps. Selling the seat was the easy decade.
  2. Agents are the growthCustomers do not need another capability tour. They need three real use cases, a quick win, and a responsible path to scale.
  3. Governance decides the ceilingHow agents get secured, measured, and placed against Security and Azure is what determines whether year two happens at all.
  4. Depth beats coverageEvery engineer owns a minor and becomes the local expert in it, so the team has somewhere to send the hard question.
